# News providers V2: implemented connectors and operating limits

Reviewed and tested on 9 September 2026. This document describes the adapters in `src/v2/providers/`; the application architecture and deployment checks are documented separately. The implementation supports full reading of selected official releases and customer-configured licensed feeds. It does not turn a publisher's consumer subscription into an API or full-text licence.

> **Working-tree status, 9 September 2026: 7 open findings (2 P1, 5 P2), 2 fixed (R2, R7).** The original review identified nine findings; its observations and commit references are preserved. R7 has since been corrected and fixture-verified; R2 passed four fully intercepted browser acceptance cases. These changes are not yet committed or rollout approval. Keep licensed feeds disconnected until the remaining P1 findings are fixed and retested. The source labels remain distinct: official-policy release, issuer disclosure, licensed news and publisher summary. No adapter imports news photographs: an image URL or a text licence is not treated as proof of image-display rights. The updated interface instead uses separately credited institution photographs as source illustrations for Fed/ECB, plus commercial connector logos for identification; it excludes government seals. These images are not claimed to depict a particular reported event. The stored body is plain text; the implementation does not reproduce page navigation, advertisements, executable HTML or arbitrary publisher pages.

The read API now includes optional `previewText` and `readingMinutes`. The preview is a literal excerpt from permitted stored body text, capped at 700 characters, or the original provider summary if no permitted body is available. It is not inferred or AI-generated. Reading time uses the complete permitted body at 200 words/minute; full bodies still load separately in the reader. Live-feed eligibility is checked before pagination: permitted nonempty body, or a summary of at least 80 characters with at least 40 alphanumeric characters beyond a repeated headline. Unavailable/headline-only rows are hidden, not deleted; the archive is unaffected. Unavailable official sources with no records are omitted from default filters and grouped under inactive settings, while optional commercial connectors remain available.

Read-only working-tree validation found 26 visible official releases, all with permitted full text: 20 Fed (284–3,364 body characters) and 6 ECB (1,827–11,172). Their feed previews contain 282–700 characters. This verifies the available sample, not full global coverage or a real-time wire.

Both bounded manual Fed/ECB syncs succeeded around 09:03 UTC on 9 September after the local V2 restart, with counts unchanged. Image attribution, source URLs and hashes are recorded separately in the MVP's `public/news-sources/README.md` and `manifest.json`: Federal Reserve Board archive photograph (2011), and © European Central Bank / Bernd Hartung archive photograph (2019). The [Fed photo download page](https://www.flickr.com/photos/federalreserve/26088200676/sizes/l/) identifies the government-work image; [ECB press-photo conditions](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/contacts/html/press_photos.en.html) require credit and prohibit alteration/distortion. Source illustrations and logos do not grant news redistribution rights.

The live sample illustrates freshness rather than promising a real-time news wire: the first Fed release was published on 4 September 2026, and the first eligible ECB institutional release was published on 21 August 2026. More recent ECB feed items were authored speeches excluded by the reuse policy. Their publication dates are preserved; fetching today does not make an older release new.

## Permission and payment

For the selected Fed and ECB materials, the implementation relies on their published reuse conditions and attribution, without a new paid API agreement. SEC provides a similar free primary-source route when reachable. These sources provide useful original announcements and disclosures, but do not replace global financial journalism or prove coverage of every portfolio company.

The three commercial connectors are currently disabled and unconfigured. In addition to fixing the P1 defects, enabling them requires provider-issued API credentials and confirmation that the customer's agreement covers display in this workspace. The product records an attestation, not automated legal verification; R1 still prevents dependable revocation across concurrent settings updates; R2 cached-page revocation has been corrected and browser-verified. Benzinga full-text display has a separate switch. A consumer login, an API key, a vendor free tier, or a successful HTTP response alone does not establish employee/customer display, full-body retention, image, redistribution or model-training rights. The adapters do not send source bodies to an AI service.

For an internal company deployment, confirm employee access and any retention restrictions. For customer-facing deployment, obtain the relevant redistribution/display rights. Connecting different customers' subscriptions requires separate tenant credentials, storage and entitlement isolation; the present connector configuration is scoped to one workspace and is not a multi-tenant licensing system.

Benzinga is the implemented candidate for licensed in-platform journalism. Its [AWS offering](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-xwgvhwowjmw3g) and linked agreement distinguish the licensed service and permitted users; the customer's actual order and licence govern display and redistribution. An applicable full-text price and quota have not been quoted or purchased. Marketaux and Finnhub are summary connectors even if a customer pays for their API plan. Vendor plans and prices must be checked against the customer's required scope; the local request budget below is not a vendor price or entitlement.

## Checkpoints, freshness and request budgets

Every actual upstream HTTP request reserves the worker's persistent PostgreSQL quota before it is sent. This includes feed downloads, release-body downloads and SEC mapping/document requests. Local defaults are 30 requests/minute for each official source, 20 for Benzinga, and 10 for each summary vendor, further bounded by the configured daily quota. These are application limits, not statements about purchased vendor quotas. SEC also has a shared per-host-family delay of 550 ms, below two requests/second, stricter than its published [10 requests/second maximum](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces).

The worker normally checks free sources every 15 minutes and enabled commercial sources every five minutes. Cadence is configurable and does not guarantee publication-to-screen latency: provider delivery, quota, queueing and outages can delay updates.

| Connector | Persisted continuation | What completion means; important limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fed / ECB | Up to 100 eligible feed entries, fingerprints of title/description/date/category/URL, body-failure/access-denial state and a daily full-scan timestamp. Each batch fetches at most 10 bodies. | Every eligible entry in the bounded current snapshot is processed across batches. New/changed entries fetch bodies at the feed-check cadence. Unchanged healthy feeds cause one RSS request and no body downloads, except the daily correction rescan. Transient failures receive up to three further attempts on subsequent feed checks, then daily checks, within budget. Body access denials do not automatically retry; an explicit checkpoint reset is required. Releases no longer in RSS are not monitored for corrections or inferred to be withdrawn. |
| SEC | Fixed pending filing metadata and the current CIK index. At most five documents per batch. Optional targeted mode accepts at most 10 CIKs and selects the latest 10 supported disclosures for each. | A bounded current-feed or targeted snapshot, not a full filings archive. Forms are 8-K/8-K/A, 6-K/6-K/A, 10-Q/10-Q/A, 10-K/10-K/A, 20-F and 40-F. Feed mode resolves the exact primary document through submissions data. SEC remains blocked here. R7 is corrected: transient document failures emit no upsert, preserving prior text; a later bounded snapshot can retry, while HTTP 401/403 pauses the worker's automatic requests. |
| Benzinga | Fixed initial update window, page offset and a news/removal phase. News uses `updatedSince` and `sort=updated:asc`; removals use the documented removal endpoint. | Both phases must finish before the stable watermark advances. Five-minute overlap repeats boundary updates. Offset pagination is not a transactional snapshot or a lossless stream. The removal offset ceiling stops the job; however, R4 shows stale replay can resurrect a withdrawn article because the store loses ordering metadata. |
| Marketaux | Fixed publication window and page offset; checks each response's `meta.limit` but does not persist the effective size in the cursor. | Completion is intended to exhaust the bounded window, but R8 demonstrates false completion after a page-size change. The adapter stops at the 20,000-result ceiling. Five-minute overlap reduces late-arrival gaps; old updates outside that overlap may be missed. No removal stream is implemented. R9 separately affects the original V1 adapter. |
| Finnhub | General-news ID watermark, or the selected symbol plus remaining full article records serialized into the cursor. | General mode is a bounded snapshot; targeted mode covers at most five symbols, not all portfolio companies. R3 shows that accepted responses can exceed the worker's cursor limit before any item is saved. No general correction/removal guarantee is inferred. |

For today's 20-item Fed feed, an unchanged 24-hour operating cycle at 15-minute cadence is about 96 RSS requests plus 20 daily body checks, plus any new/changed entries and bounded exceptional retries. This avoids downloading all 20 bodies on every poll. Failed retrieval preserves previously imported text even if RSS metadata changed. Three additional transient retries occur on subsequent feed checks, then on daily rescans; no tight retry loop or access-block workaround is used. Older checkpoints without fingerprints intentionally trigger one fresh body scan when migrated.

The backend keeps a resume cursor for incomplete jobs and advances the stable provider checkpoint when the adapter reports a completed window. That condition is not proof of complete delivery: R3 prevents persistence and R8 can report completion with missing items. A default job processes at most three adapter batches and becomes partial if more work remains. A job-level authentication/access failure is reported with its sanitized HTTP status and pauses automatic retries until settings are saved or the operator requests a manual fetch. Fed/ECB body-level access denials are separately persisted in the feed checkpoint and require an explicit checkpoint reset before that body is attempted again. No VPN, alternative identity, request-header rotation or paywall workaround is implemented.

## Company identity and scale

The default strategy ingests a provider feed once, then associates returned entities with the company directory. It does not fetch the same global feed separately for 1,500, 10,000 or 50,000 companies. A five-minute per-company polling design would require approximately 432,000, 2.88 million or 14.4 million requests/day respectively, before pagination; those are arithmetic workload estimates, not vendor capacity claims.

Marketaux observations preserve name, ticker, exchange, country and a provider identifier. Benzinga's documented `stocks[].name` field is a ticker, **not a company name**; the adapter maps it to ticker and preserves any supplied exchange and ISIN. SEC preserves the CIK, accession, and available issuer name/listing evidence. The strict directory resolver can use supplied exchange/name evidence and leaves weak or ambiguous matches unresolved. It does not assume every symbol belongs to a US listing.

Finnhub's current targeted adapter records the requested ticker as query provenance. Its company-news response does not itself establish an exchange or canonical company ID, so the bare ticker is not enough for a verified directory association. A verified symbol-to-listing mapping is needed before relying on Finnhub company-news in portfolio filters. Official macro releases are not assigned to companies based on incidental text mentions. No connector test establishes complete coverage of the 50,000-company target.

Catalogue changes also require a repair path: R6 shows that an unchanged-content replay does not rebuild article-company links after the previously missing company becomes resolvable. This is separate from Finnhub's documented missing identity evidence; both must be addressed before claiming dependable portfolio coverage as the directory grows.

For the next licensed rollout, obtain a feed entitlement with the required geographic/company scope, validate exchange-qualified entity mappings on a representative portfolio, confirm permitted users and article retention, and measure source latency and gaps with that actual account. LSEG/Reuters and Dow Jones remain separate enterprise procurement candidates, not implemented adapters. [LSEG's news product overview](https://developers.lseg.com/en/product/news/overview) distinguishes programmatic and display usage, so an analytics feed should not be assumed to include a human article reader.

## Safety and verification

The fetcher uses a fixed HTTPS host/path allowlist, denies redirects, enforces a 15-second request timeout and streamed byte limits, and calls the durable request-budget callback for every real network request. Official body HTML is converted to paragraphs of plain text. Unknown layouts are not imported as article text; existing good text is preserved, and newly observed headline-only entries fail the live-feed readability filter. SEC uses an honest project User-Agent with `https://github.com/1nsar/investin-com-news-service`, not an invented contact address.

Provider keys remain server-side and are never returned by the adapters; reflected literal or URL-encoded API credentials are redacted from returned data. API URLs containing keys and raw response bodies are not included in operational error messages. Paid connection tests do not create subscriptions, trades, purchases or vendor accounts.

Pre-review validation for these adapters: **20 focused tests passed**, covering real source URL boundaries, heading-versus-body extraction, unavailable content, persistent request accounting, feed continuation, metadata changes/daily rescans, SEC exact-document mapping, Marketaux actual plan page size, Benzinga full-text gating/removals, update-versus-correction labels, Finnhub symbol continuation and credential redaction. Those original cases did not cover the later findings: testing a fixed page size does not verify a plan change; testing a removal alone does not verify stale replay after it. A Benzinga publication/update timestamp difference is displayed as an update; an editorial correction label requires an explicit boolean source marker. Bounded live requests verified Fed and ECB feeds and selected bodies; SEC access failed with HTTP 403. Commercial payload tests use fixtures, and do not verify the customer's future API entitlement or actual commercial feed delivery.

Subsequent working-tree validation passed **24 focused provider tests**, **7 PostgreSQL rollback tests**, TypeScript and whitespace checks. New cases cover R7's success → HTTP 503 → recovery sequence, preservation after changed official RSS metadata, three-retry bounds, no automatic retries after body access denial, readable-feed cursor pagination and preview licensing. The full service suite subsequently passed 131 offline tests; 19 Polymarket regression tests and frontend TypeScript/ESLint also passed. Four fully intercepted browser cases verified the R2 correction, with no JavaScript errors. R2 and R7 are fixed; seven findings remain open. Original review observations and screenshots remain preserved in the evidence register.
